# OpenTan rules

OpenTan follows the standard rules of the classic trading-and-building board
game. This page is the reference for what the app enforces, so a rules argument
at the table can be settled without leaving the room.

## The board

- Two to four players use 19 tiles: 4 forest, 4 pasture, 4 fields, 3 hills,
  3 mountains and 1 desert, with the number tokens
  2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 8, 8, 9, 9, 10, 10, 11, 11, 12.
- Five or six players use 30 tiles: 6 forest, 6 pasture, 6 fields, 5 hills,
  5 mountains and 2 deserts, with 28 tokens.
- The robber starts on a desert, which never produces.
- Nine ports on the small board and eleven on the large one sit on pairs of
  coastal corners: one 2:1 port for each resource, the rest 3:1.
- By default the board is reshuffled until no two tiles bearing a 6 or an 8
  touch. This is a house rule and can be turned off.

## Opening placement

In seating order, each player places a settlement and then a road touching it.
The order then reverses and each player places a second settlement and road. The
second settlement immediately pays one resource for each tile around it. The
distance rule applies from the first placement; the road connection rule does
not.

## A turn

1. Optionally play one development card. A knight may be played before the roll.
2. Roll two dice. Every tile showing that number pays each player with a building
   on one of its corners: one resource per settlement, two per city. The tile
   under the robber pays nothing.
3. Build, trade and play a development card in any order, then end your turn.

Only one development card may be played per turn, and never on the turn it was
bought.

### Sevens

Nobody collects. Every player holding more than the hand limit (seven by default)
discards half, rounded down. The player who rolled then moves the robber to a
different tile and steals one random resource from a player with a building on
that tile.

## Building

| | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Road | 1 brick, 1 lumber |
| Settlement | 1 brick, 1 lumber, 1 wool, 1 grain |
| City | 2 grain, 3 ore |
| Development card | 1 wool, 1 grain, 1 ore |

Each player has 15 roads, 5 settlements and 4 cities. Upgrading a settlement to a
city returns the settlement piece to your supply.

- A settlement needs an empty corner with all three neighbouring corners empty,
  and after the opening it must touch one of your own roads.
- A road must touch one of your own roads or buildings. Another player's
  settlement or city breaks the connection through that corner.
- A city replaces one of your own settlements.

## Development cards

The small board's deck holds 25 cards: 14 knights, 5 victory points, 2 road
building, 2 year of plenty and 2 monopoly. The large board's deck is larger in
the same proportions.

- **Knight** — move the robber and steal, as on a seven. Counts towards the
  largest army.
- **Road building** — place two roads for free.
- **Year of plenty** — take any two resources from the bank.
- **Monopoly** — name a resource and take every card of it from every other
  player.
- **Victory point** — one point, kept hidden until you win.

## Trading

On your turn you may trade with any other player at the table, and with the bank
at four of one resource for one of another. A 3:1 port improves that to three for
one and a 2:1 port to two for one of the named resource; you must have a
settlement or city on one of the port's two corners. The special build phase in
the five and six player game allows building and buying, but not trading.

## The bank

The bank holds 19 of each resource on the small board and 24 on the large one. If
it cannot pay a resource in full and more than one player is owed it, nobody
receives that resource. If only one player is owed it, they take what is left.

## Winning

Settlements are worth one point and cities two. The longest road and the largest
army are worth two each. The longest road is awarded at five connected segments
and the largest army at three played knights; each passes to whoever overtakes
the current holder, and a tie leaves the card where it is. The target is ten
points by default and can be changed when starting a game. You can only win on
your own turn, because victory point cards are revealed then.
